Executive Summary

This empirical capstone investigation explores the relationship between fine-grained retail investor emotions expressed on social media and stock market movements across 37 major equities and the S&P 500 index throughout 2020. Evaluating four core pillars—Real-World Case Studies, Econometric Research, Machine Learning Model Development, and Software Engineering—the study establishes a fundamental academic distinction: investor emotion acts as an instantaneous, contemporaneous mirror of market movements rather than a causal predictor of forward returns.

1. Data Architecture & Integrity Verification

The research utilized 10,000 verified posts across 37 equities from train_stockemo.csv (8,000), val_stockemo.csv (1,000), and test_stockemo.csv (1,000), paired with 41 price series from dataset/price/.

  • Missing Values & Duplicates: Zero missing cells (0/10,000) and zero duplicated posts across IDs or text.
  • Text Length IQR Bounds: Median character length of 66 chars (IQR bound: 174.5 chars, 4.95% outliers). Median word count of 13 words (IQR bound: 34 words, 4.23% outliers).
  • Emoji Coverage: 100.0% of posts contain expressive emotional emojis.
  • Return Outliers (|r| > 10%): 185 occurrences out of 9,375 trading sessions (1.97%), led by Carnival's +39.29% surge on Nov 9, 2020.

3. Real-World Case Studies Based Learning

Case 1: The March 2020 COVID-19 Liquidity Shock & Panic Peak

During the historic 34% drawdown of the S&P 500, negative emotion posts surged by 124% to 443 posts in March. Retail bullish sentiment bottomed at an annual low of 46.8%. However, peak retail panic coincided precisely with the market trough (March 23, 2020 at 2,237), which was followed by the fastest recovery rally in market history.

Case 2: The November 2020 "Vaccine Monday" Relief Rally

On November 9, 2020, Pfizer announced >90% vaccine efficacy. Reopening equities like Carnival (CCL) surged +39.29% in a single day (the maximum return in the dataset). Investor emotion flipped instantly from depression and panic to excitement and belief, pricing in multi-year recovery expectations in a single session.

Case 3: The Retail Trading Explosion & Tesla (TSLA) Euphoria

Tesla alone accounted for 4,341 of the 10,000 tweets (43.4%), illustrating severe power-law volume concentration. Maintained across 64.2% bullish sentiment, retail excitement drove a +743% price run in 2020, demonstrating how retail social momentum can create substantial reflexivity.

Chronological Out-of-Sample Market Direction Prediction

In a strict chronological split (80% Train, N=7,967 / 20% Test, N=1,992), machine learning models using emotion features achieved 51.00% accuracy, failing to outperform the 52.56% Majority Baseline. This provides rigorous empirical proof of the Weak-Form Efficient Market Hypothesis.

6. Conclusion

This capstone demonstrates that natural language processing can effectively quantify investor psychological states from noisy social discourse. However, financial markets efficiently absorb this information in real time: social media sentiment serves as an exceptional instantaneous mirror of market health, rather than an oracle of future stock returns.
